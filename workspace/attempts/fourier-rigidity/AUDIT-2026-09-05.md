## L8b: recheck and a second route

The repaired Step C is sound on re-reading: the `3/4` window is finite by
the counting bound; `|h|<1/4` makes every tail rate strictly positive;
`|h|<|y_0|` makes the chosen nonreal rate have negative real part; excluding
finitely many h values isolates its coefficient. The finite exponential
sum is extracted in increasing real-rate order. Cesàro averaging eliminates
the remaining distinct imaginary frequencies. A real atom is isolated by
the unique zero rate with h=0. No uniform zero separation is needed.

Step B's initial claim about density in the full weighted sup-norm space
was too broad: cutoff convergence is true for the little-o subspace used
by its Gaussian tests. L8 now defines that subspace from the outset.
Step A can be shortened to evenization and complexification; modulation
and Fourier synthesis are unnecessary for a symmetric multiset.

An independent *derivation route*, by the same agent, avoids Gaussian
extraction altogether. For the difference of two symmetric strip multisets
whose compact-smooth test sums vanish, use the mollification/cutoff bounds
of L9 §2 to extend the equality to `e^{-a|u|}`, for every real `a>1/2`.
Only the zero-sum bound (2) is needed here, not positivity or S3. Thus

```
R_Z(a)=R_Z′(a),
R_Z(x)=m_Z(0)/x + Σ_{pairs {z,-z}, z≠0} 2x/(x²+z²).
```

The paired series is normally convergent off its locally finite poles
because `Σ|z|^{-2}<∞`. It defines a meromorphic function on C.
Equality on `(1/2,∞)` implies equality meromorphically. At `x=iz≠0`
the residue is the multiplicity of z, while the residue at 0 is `m_Z(0)`.
Equality of residues identifies the multisets. This verifies L8b's conclusion
by a different analytic mechanism without assuming real zeros.

This is not a blind second reviewer or an independent-agent review. The
historical 2026-07-12 reports remain historical, not new verification.

## Additional adversarial pass after the cycle draft

The following potential failures were checked explicitly:

- **Hidden central parity:** S1 permits an odd multiplicity at 0. L9 retains
  the sign `ε=(-1)^m_0`, applies the two-function theorem to F and εF, and
  derives ε=1 afterward. Assuming an even numerator at the start would be wrong.
- **Hidden coefficient axiom:** translated tests have fixed width in log n;
  nonnegativity bounds their total weights without assuming individual decay
  or prime-power support. The resulting geometric sum gives the needed
  absolute convergence; no Booker or Selberg coefficient axiom is imported.
- **Origin singularity:** the second derivative of the exponential test is
  a finite signed measure, not an L1 function before mollification. L9 uses
  weighted total variation and bounds the regularized W integrand separately.
- **Infinite-product growth:** paired products and reciprocal gamma both
  have explicit `O(r log r)` logarithmic maximum bounds. No unproved vertical
  growth condition is smuggled into Hamburger's hypotheses.
- **Numerical reality bias:** a new toy-divisor check includes ±(2±0.3i),
  repeated atoms at ±3, and central multiplicities 1 and 2. Direct Fourier
  integration and differentiation of the product agree within 1.3e-35;
  completion parity agrees within 1.6e-36 at the selected points. These
  finite toys test identities, not membership of S1–S3 or RH.

No gap was found in this same-agent pass. It does not remove the need for
an independent review. L9 does not depend on the independent-verification
status of L8b because its reconstruction does not use that lemma.

## Corrected logical and terminology issues

- L8c forbids a **distinct finite modification**, not a nonreal member equal
  to ζ. The previous corollary accidentally excluded the possibility RH is false.
- Fixed-comb uniqueness and finite-defect rigidity do not establish zero reality.
  With fixed data, their DH analogues are harmless uniqueness statements too.
- The atomic term is **minus** a comb with nonnegative weights. W_∞ is
  singular at 0. The object is an explicit-formula functional, not a standard
  Fourier quasicrystal or a positive diffraction measure.
- An off-axis atom defines evaluation on entire transforms; it is not an
  ordinary distributional Fourier transform of a measure on R. No unconditional
  temperedness of the entire zero functional is used.
- Merely matching ζ's degree and density does not put a system in the Selberg
  class, establish Grand RH, or justify importing a classification theorem.
- L8's wider-test remark lacked sufficient stated convergence hypotheses;
  it has been replaced by a reference to the explicitly justified extensions.

Booker [Booker2015], Definition 1.3 (A1), requires for `f(n)=w_n` both
`f(n)log^k n=O_k(1)` for every k and `Σ_{n≤X}|f(n)|²=O_ε(X^ε)`.
The translated-bump estimate alone does not establish these. His positivity
means finitely many *negative divisor multiplicities*, not positive comb
coefficients; subtracting ζ's two pole atoms is compatible with that notion.
His Theorem 1.7 is therefore not a shortcut from S1–S3. The new proof uses
Hamburger's theorem instead. After singleton identification the coefficient
conditions follow from ζ, but using that earlier would be circular.
